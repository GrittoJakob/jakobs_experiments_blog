MPO Results: Experiments and Results

This post presents the experimental results of my MPO implementation for continuous-control tasks, in meta-reiforcment learning and multi-task settings. We compare the results of the MPO algorithm to the resulsts of a PPO baseline and Rl²-PPO version, which my team-college in our project for university implemented.

Previous post. This post builds directly on the theory and implementation details discussed in MPO Algorithm: Theory and Implementation Details.

Overview

As mentioned in my previous post, this preject started as part of an lecture at TUM. Me and my teammate, both experimented to facilitate the sim-2-real transfer by meta-reinforcement learning. While I implemented the MPO and RL²-MPO, he worked on the RL²-PPO and PPO as a baseline algorithm. Then, we compared our algorithms in different tasks: meta-reinforcement learning and robusts multi-task both regarding the performance and sample efficiency. While the RL²-MPO version remains promising, it suffers from very runtime costs, thats why it was not possible to train the algorithm robustly. Experimental Setup

All experiments were run with the MPO implementation described in the previous post. Unless otherwise stated, the same general training pipeline, optimizer settings, and replay-buffer setup were used across experiments.

  • Environment: Ant-v5
  • Batch size: 1024
  • Replay buffer size: 1000000
  • Number of action samples in E-step: 20
  • Evaluation metric: episodic return

Robust-Multitask Experiments

Setting

We trained our policies to run to different target goals which which were randomly sampled during training. As a task-hint we provided the nomilized distance to goal and the sin(alpha) cos(alpha) angles to goal. Additionally to reaching different goals, we injected random noise (ERFI) to the joints of the ant. [One sentence to ERFI benefits: easy to implement and less effort than domain randomization]. For evaluation we used to different maneuvers, which our ants had to run under different ERFI noise level. Another design choice is whether tanh is applied to the mean of the Gaussian policy. This changes how actions are constrained and may influence both exploration and early optimization.
